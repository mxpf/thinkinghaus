The name makes Thinkinghaus sound more organized than it is.

This is mostly a place for things I can’t quite stop looking at: people, websites, books, movies, places, tools, ideas, design, odd communities, things somebody has been making for thirty years, and whatever else seems worth paying attention to for a little longer than necessary.

I’m a designer, so design will inevitably show up here. Sometimes it’ll even be the whole subject. But Thinkinghaus isn’t a design blog, and I don’t particularly want every walk, book, strange website, or spiritual experience to eventually explain what it taught me about creativity. Some things can just be themselves.

What I’m trying to practice here is something closer to beginner’s mind: encountering a thing before everything I already know tells me what it is. I’m not especially good at this. Expertise, taste, politics, irritation, and ego are all remarkably quick on their feet. But I’ve found that when I can get them out of the doorway for a minute, the world usually gets more interesting.

I’m especially drawn to places where you can feel another person on the other side. Personal websites. Niche cultures. Long-running obsessions. People who are really, genuinely into something. Not because outsiders are automatically right, handmade things are automatically good, or sincerity excuses anything. It doesn’t. I just like encountering the shape of somebody else’s attention.

Sometimes that produces admiration. Sometimes criticism. Sometimes I find something I strongly disagree with and want to understand how anybody got there. The hope is to stay interested long enough that judgment isn’t the only thing I bring home.

The site is simple on purpose. Not because white space is more virtuous than color or because I’ve taken vows in some austere typographic monastery. I want as little machinery as possible between finding something interesting, thinking about it, and putting it somewhere another person can encounter it. The emptiness leaves room for whatever happens to arrive next.

That also keeps the place provisional. I don’t want Thinkinghaus to become a persona I have to protect. I expect to contradict myself, change my mind, get things wrong, write something very serious beside something completely unnecessary, and occasionally wonder why I thought any of this was a good idea.

These pieces are developed in collaboration with AI. That collaboration can involve long conversations, questions, research, argument, interpretation, drafting, and revision. I don’t think authorship is usefully reduced to who physically typed each sentence. The more important questions for me are whose encounter with the world this is, whether the ideas feel like mine, and whether I’m willing to take responsibility for them. If I’m not, we keep working.

There isn’t a particularly grand objective beyond making something worth encountering. Maybe it’s useful. Maybe it produces understanding, wonder, delight, friction, laughter, or a question that sticks around. Maybe it’s just a good link you wouldn’t have found otherwise.

Leave the place a little better than you found it, then keep looking.
