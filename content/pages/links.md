Places I return to, things I’ve recently discovered, and corners of the internet I don’t want to lose.

This isn’t a canon and it’s definitely not an endorsement of every idea contained inside. They’re here because something about them made me want to keep looking.

[Cabinet](https://cabinetmagazine.org/)

A magazine built on the pleasant assumption that almost anything becomes interesting if you look at it hard enough. Extremely good at wandering across the borders between art, science, history, objects, and ideas without worrying too much about which department owns them.

[Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/)

I met Emily briefly years ago, but I’d already known her through interactive fiction. Her writing about narrative systems, characters, games, and how stories actually behave has been a rabbit hole for me ever since.

[Hundred Rabbits](https://100r.ca/)

Rekka and Devine live and work aboard a sailboat, making software, tools, games, drawings, manuals, and an ongoing record of how they live. Their line “That which cannot be repaired is already broken” has been stuck in my head. This feels lived rather than lifestyle-branded.

[The Center for Land Use Interpretation](https://clui.org/)

A research organization devoted to noticing what we’ve done with the land. Missile ranges, mines, infrastructure, waste sites, water systems, borders. The places we usually drive past become very strange once somebody actually points at them.

[Marginalia Search](https://search.marginalia.nu/)

An independent search engine built to surface the small, text-heavy, noncommercial web that larger search engines tend to bury. I started using it after putting this page together and now visit it constantly. Search that still feels a little like wandering.

[Tiny Awards](https://tinyawards.net/)

An annual celebration of small, handmade, noncommercial websites. I’m obsessed with the range: serious political work sitting a few clicks away from somebody who decided the internet needed a rotating sandwich. Correct.

[The Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/)

An old favorite. Strange books, forgotten illustrations, scientific diagrams, photographs, occult material, marginalia, and whatever else has escaped copyright and wandered into the commons. Almost irresponsibly easy to lose an afternoon here.

[Whole Earth Index](https://www.wholeearth.info/)

A nearly complete archive of the publications that grew out of the Whole Earth Catalog. Self-sufficiency, alternative technology, counterculture, tools, education, ecology, and several decades of people asking what might be possible if they did things differently. A gold mine.

[Scope of Work](https://www.scopeofwork.net/)

Spencer Wright’s long-running writing about manufacturing, engineering, infrastructure, maintenance, and the physical world. I like how seriously it takes the people who actually know how things get built and kept running.

[Works That Work](https://worksthatwork.com/)

A finished magazine, which is perhaps part of its charm now. “Unexpected creativity” was its territory: the ingenuity buried inside ordinary systems, objects, communities, and places rather than design as a parade of expensive chairs.

[Low-tech Magazine](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/)

Research into older, simpler, overlooked ways of solving problems, published on a website designed around the same questions. I especially like projects where the philosophy leaks into the plumbing.

[A Working Library](https://aworkinglibrary.com/)

Mandy Brown reading across books, technology, work, politics, and whatever else attaches itself along the way. The library is “working” in the literal sense: one thing gets connected to another and starts producing new thought.

[The Simplicity Collective](https://www.simplicitycollective.com/)

An earnest, unfashionably serious exploration of living with less in a culture built around wanting more. I don’t need every conclusion to land for me to appreciate somebody actually asking the question at full volume.

[Kagi](https://kagi.com/)

A search engine you pay for instead of one financed by selling your attention. More importantly, it’s just a much nicer way to search: tunable, quiet, and unusually good at getting me somewhere useful. This became a daily visit almost immediately.
