I have never been especially good at bookmarks. Things end up in open tabs, notes, screenshots, messages to myself, and occasionally in the part of my brain apparently reserved for URLs from 2003.

This is an attempt to keep a few of them somewhere more useful.

These are places I return to, places I expect to return to, and places that have sent me somewhere I would not have found by searching for what I thought I wanted. Some are resources. Some are people. Some are entire little worlds.

The categories are approximate.

*Inhabited sites*

[Hundred Rabbits](https://100r.co/)

Rekka Bellum and Devine Lu Linvega make software, games, books, music, tools, and other things while living aboard a sailboat and thinking seriously about technological resilience. Their line—“That which cannot be repaired is already broken”—has been lodged in my head since I found it.

There is a lot here.

[A Working Library](https://aworkinglibrary.com/)

Mandy Brown’s ongoing library of reading, work, technology, and thought. I like that the site feels accumulated rather than produced. Books lead to notes, notes lead elsewhere, and the whole thing remains visibly in use.

[Scope of Work](https://www.scopeofwork.net/)

Work in its unusually broad sense: tools, machinery, maintenance, competence, labor, curiosity, and the strange reasons people become interested in doing difficult things well.

[Low-tech Magazine](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/)

A long-running investigation of older technologies, forgotten solutions, energy use, resilience, and the assumption that newer necessarily means better.

The solar-powered website is part of the argument, which I appreciate. A philosophy becomes more interesting when it has to pay its own electricity bill.

[Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/)

Writing about interactive fiction, narrative systems, games, AI, conversation, storytelling tools, and the mechanics hiding underneath all of them.

I met Emily years ago through the interactive-fiction world. I was already an admirer. The depth of the archive has only gotten more unreasonable since.

[Zarfhome](https://eblong.com/)

Andrew Plotkin’s home on the web: interactive fiction, programming, books, games, tools, experiments, archives, and decades of things that seemed worth keeping.

I first arrived through his games more than twenty years ago and never really left.

*Deep shelves*

[Cabinet](https://www.cabinetmagazine.org/)

Art, philosophy, science, history, architecture, odd objects, forgotten ideas, and extremely specific questions treated with enough seriousness to become even stranger.

One of my favorite kinds of publication: intellectually rigorous without behaving as though curiosity needs departmental permission.

[The Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/)

A beautifully curated entrance into the enormous public-domain archive: art, literature, science, diagrams, mysticism, forgotten books, peculiar historical obsessions.

Already a longtime favorite. I rarely leave by the same door I came in.

[Whole Earth Index](https://wholeearth.info/)

The Whole Earth publications, gathered into one enormous searchable archive.

Tools, ecology, alternative technology, self-sufficiency, architecture, counterculture, computers, farming, education, weirdness. A gold mine, although “mine” implies someone has already separated the useful material from the rocks.

[Works That Work](https://worksthatwork.com/)

A magazine about unexpected examples of design and ingenuity, particularly outside the places where professional design culture normally goes looking for them.

The archive is finished now, which somehow makes it feel even more like a book you can wander around inside.

[The Simplicity Collective](https://simplicitycollective.com/)

Voluntary simplicity considered as something larger than decluttering: consumption, ecology, community, time, meaning, work, and the question of what enough might actually mean.

I like simplicity much more when it creates room for life than when it becomes another thing to optimize.

[The Center for Land Use Interpretation](https://clui.org/)

A research organization devoted to looking at how human beings use and alter the landscape.

Infrastructure, industrial sites, strange boundaries, extraction, transportation, military landscapes, overlooked structures—the ordinary physical evidence of what a society actually does, rather than what it says it does.

*Ways out*

[Marginalia Search](https://marginalia-search.com/)

An independent search engine built to surface the smaller, older, stranger, more text-heavy web.

Finding this felt less like discovering another search engine than finding a side entrance that had accidentally been left unlocked.

[Kagi Small Web](https://kagi.com/smallweb/)

A stream of writing from independent sites around the web.

This became useful approximately five minutes after I discovered it.

[Tiny Awards](https://tinyawards.net/)

An annual celebration of small, handmade, independent websites.

This can mean an extraordinarily thoughtful political visualization.

It can also mean rotating sandwiches.

Both seem important.

This page owes its basic idea to [47nil’s links page](https://47nil.com/links), though the filing system has already begun to wander.

I expect it to change.
