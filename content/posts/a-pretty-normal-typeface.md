Untitled Sans began as an attempt to make another typeface plainer.

Kris Sowersby removed some of its more noticeable details, got a little lost, put it away, returned to it years later, and nearly deleted it. Reading about the Super Normal project eventually helped him understand why a deliberately ordinary typeface might be worth finishing.

I like that the reasoning came later.

Design processes are often written up as though every decision followed neatly from the one before it. Mine rarely work that way. I try things, change my mind, forget why I started, and occasionally end up with something useful.

The explanation usually becomes clear once the work is almost done.

Sowersby describes removing traces of his own recognizable style from the letters. The goal was not to make the typeface distinctive. It was to make something that could be used without bringing too much baggage along with it.

That is harder than it sounds.

Designers are generally encouraged to make things memorable. A plain typeface can feel like a missed opportunity. But most of the time, I do not need the typeface to be the most interesting thing on the page.

I need it to help me read.

That is probably why Untitled Sans feels right for this site. The writing can wander around and follow unusual ideas. The letters mostly stay put.

Of course, Untitled Sans is still designed. Its plainness is the result of many small choices. It just does not ask me to think about those choices every time I read a paragraph.

There is something appealing about making a thing that works well without constantly advertising how much work went into it.

It does not need to disappear completely.

It just knows when to be quiet.
