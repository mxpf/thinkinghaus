When I chose a word for [my translation of the Gītā](https://keeping.haus/gita/), I could usually say what drew me to it. Sometimes several words fit the Sanskrit equally well, but one was [easier to say aloud](https://keeping.haus/gita/translation-charter/#aloud). It was still up to me to decide if the spoken word factor was enough to tip the decision.

I kept thinking about this while reading Geir Isene’s remarkably thoughtful [work on free will](https://isene.com/freewill/). He asks whether choice could account for the existence of the world itself.

What gives me pause is how little of my ordinary sense of *choosing* carries over. If nothing favored this particular world, I still want to know why this is the one we got.

My study of Advaita Vedānta leaves me wondering whether the world needs a chooser at all. I can pay attention to a decision as I make it. I find it much harder to say [what awareness itself is doing](https://keeping.haus/gita/commentary/#verse-13-29) in that moment. Does it choose the word? Or is it what lets me hear the difference?
