On an unusually warm weekday in April, I committed a small crime of opportunity.

I was between projects—the quiet side of a feast-or-famine business—and work had not claimed the day yet. Before it could, I drove north to Minnewaska State Park Preserve.

I knew parts of Severance had been filmed there. That was enough of a plan.

I like road trips, being outside, and finding places close to home that I have somehow missed. I was also curious about the show’s outdoor world. It feels bleak, Gothic, and slightly unreal. I wanted to see how much of that feeling belonged to the locations themselves.

At the gate, someone took my ticket. I saw a few visitors in the lodge. After that, no one.

There were miles of carriage roads, bare trees, open rock, water, and a large amount of sky. I walked toward the waterfall with the strange feeling that I had borrowed the whole preserve for the afternoon.

I expected it to feel eerie because of the show. It did not.

It felt quiet and restorative. The place was beautiful, but it was not waiting around to be Severance. The color, framing, weather, and story had done much of that work.

I liked seeing the gap between the real place and the fictional one.

At one of the locations, I could see several paths a camera might have taken. There was no inevitable shot sitting there. Someone had walked around, looked at the rock and water, and decided what the place could become.

That made the scene more interesting to me, not less.

It reminded me that looking is not simply finding the unusual thing. Sometimes it is noticing an unusual possibility inside something that already exists.

I had no useful objective for the day. I wanted to get out of my routine and let something happen. This is usually a good enough reason for me to go somewhere.

I try to find a small edge I am slightly reluctant to cross. Take the unclaimed weekday. Drive without much of a plan. Walk alone. Step across the water. One edge tends to lead to another.

Chance matters in my work for the same reason. I bring in another person, an unfamiliar reference, a random constraint, or AI because I want something to enter that I could not have planned by myself.

On a cover design for Sands, I was looking for a natural image that could sit beside an architectural detail with a curling form. AI produced a picture of fiddleheads. Their curves mirrored the architecture and accidentally formed an S.

I would not have planned it. I still had to notice it.

Chance can place something in front of me. It cannot make me care or decide what to do with it.

The trip offered no guarantee either. I went to a park, walked to a waterfall, and came home.

This essay is one of the things that happened.

Maybe that is what I mean by remaining strange enough to notice: leaving enough room in the day, and in myself, for something unplanned to get in.
