Does the world need a chooser?

November 20, 2026

1 minute read

Edit

When I chose a word for my translation of the Gītā, I could usually say what drew me to it. Sometimes several words fit the Sanskrit equally well, but one was easier to say aloud. It was still up to me to decide if the spoken word factor was enough to tip the decision.

I kept thinking about this while reading Geir Isene’s remarkably thoughtful work on free will. He asks whether choice could account for the existence of the world itself.

What gives me pause is how little of my ordinary sense of choosing carries over. If nothing favored this particular world, I still want to know why this is the one we got.

My study of Advaita Vedānta leaves me wondering whether the world needs a chooser at all. I can pay attention to a decision as I make it. I find it much harder to say what awareness itself is doing in that moment. Does it choose the word? Or is it what lets me hear the difference?
