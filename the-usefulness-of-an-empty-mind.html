The Usefulness of an Empty Mind

August 6, 2026

2 minutes

Zen Mind, Beginner’s Mind begins with a simple distinction.

In the beginner’s mind there are many possibilities. In the expert’s, there are few.

I have probably spent most of my life trying to acquire the expert’s mind.

Expertise is useful. It recognizes patterns, avoids old mistakes, and knows which details matter. It can also become a kind of compression. The more I know about something, the less of it I may actually see.

I see the category before the thing.

Shunryu Suzuki does not suggest becoming ignorant. Beginner’s mind is not the absence of experience. It is a way of holding experience without letting it harden around us.

This is more difficult than it sounds.

The book is assembled from talks and still feels spoken. Ideas appear, disappear, and return slightly changed. Sometimes an explanation leaves me less certain than when it began.

That seems appropriate.

The book does not provide a system for mastering Zen. It keeps removing the part of me that wants to master it.

Suzuki writes about posture, breathing, repetition, effort, and the ordinary difficulty of sitting still. The spiritual life he describes is surprisingly physical. It is in the hands, the spine, and the willingness to sit without turning the experience into an achievement.

I find this both reassuring and irritating.

I like practices that produce results. I want to know whether I am progressing. Even when trying to become less ambitious, I can become ambitious about becoming less ambitious.

Suzuki seems quietly amused by this problem.

His answer is to return to the activity itself. Sit because you are sitting. Breathe because you are breathing. Do the thing without constantly stepping outside it to decide what it means about you.

I cannot say I consistently live this way.

But I recognize the freedom in it.

Beginner’s mind may be less about innocence than repeatedly setting down what I think I know. Not throwing it away. Just loosening my grip long enough for something else to appear.

The book does not make Zen easy.

It makes simplicity seem like enough to work on.
